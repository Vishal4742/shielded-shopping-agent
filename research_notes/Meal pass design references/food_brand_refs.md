# Home meal design references

## First-party references for home-style meals and subscriptions

### Takeaway
The most relevant references are Indian tiffin services and a few polished prepared-meal brands. Their strongest reusable patterns are local kitchen discovery, specific meal and plan information, and a short “how it works” flow; their business claims should not be treated as verified facts.

### Cited Findings
- [Tumyto](https://tumyto.com/) leads with a city/provider availability action, a short benefit list, sample meal imagery, and a process that explains choosing meals, scheduling, skipping, and delivery. It labels imagery as representative and states that actual dishes, prices, and availability depend on provider/address.
- [Chatori](https://chatori.app/) separates the customer and home-cook journeys with “I want to eat” and “I want to cook” actions, then gives each side its own three-step flow. Its sample customer listings include provider name, cuisine, rating, and per-day price; its pricing section states its usage fee and clarifies that it does not handle food, payments, or delivery.
- [Maa Ka Khana](https://www.maakakhana.in/) presents home cooks with a name, neighborhood, cuisine, cooking history, verification badge, specialty, and dishes. Its menu examples include the meal name, cook, contents, portion price, rating, and an order action.
- [Maa Ka Khana subscriptions](https://www.maakakhana.in/subscriptions) displays plan totals, meal counts, per-meal prices, included menu, skip allowance, cutoff time, and delivery terms together. Its “How Meal Subscriptions Work” section follows the same sequence as a customer’s decision: choose plan/date, receive meal, skip when plans change.
- [CookUnity](https://www.cookunity.com/) foregrounds plated food and chef provenance. A chef profile can pair a person’s background and cooking point of view with a signature dish and ratings. Its [how-it-works page](https://www.cookunity.com/how-it-works) turns plan choice, meal choice, delivery, and reuse/skip options into numbered stages, with a relevant image for each.
- [Thistle](https://www.thistle.co/how/) uses a compact staged flow for plan setup, menu selection, delivery, and plan changes, then makes its returnable delivery bags part of the service story. The page uses images to show browsing menus, arranging delivered meals, and returning packaging.

### Inferences
- For Tiffin Pass, use Tumyto and Chatori for the local-kitchen and delivery model, Maa Ka Khana for plan/price scannability, and CookUnity or Thistle for food-first photography and a clear service sequence.
- A strong Tiffin Pass price module could make the existing figures legible at a glance: total paid, number of lunches, price per lunch, delivery schedule, and the rule for unused meals.
- Kitchen profiles can build trust through concrete details (location, dishes, preparation method, delivery area) instead of generic “homemade with love” language.

### Gaps
- This review inspected page copy and structure through text extraction; it did not reliably capture exact typography, colors, spacing, or mobile screenshots. Treat these as content/UX references and inspect the visual pages directly before borrowing visual details.
- The brands’ own claims about hygiene, verification, customer ratings, awards, and service performance were not independently checked. Use these pages as design references, not evidence that a particular operating model or claim is safe or effective.

## Patterns that can improve the current design test

### Takeaway
The current page already has a strong delivery timeline and an interactive “view as” receipt. The best additions from these references are clearer proof of who prepares the food, a single price breakdown, and menu/delivery details placed where people choose a plan.

### Cited Findings
- Chatori puts customer and cook actions near the start, and its sample kitchen list shows cook, cuisine, rating, and daily price together ([Chatori](https://chatori.app/)).
- Maa Ka Khana puts meal contents and a portion price beside the food and cook details, and its plan page exposes meal count, price per meal, cutoff, and delivery terms in the plan itself ([Maa Ka Khana](https://www.maakakhana.in/), [subscriptions](https://www.maakakhana.in/subscriptions)).
- CookUnity combines a real chef’s profile with a named signature meal and rating, then presents its service stages from plan to delivery ([CookUnity](https://www.cookunity.com/), [How it works](https://www.cookunity.com/how-it-works)).
- Tumyto marks meal photography as representative and says that actual menus and pricing depend on location/provider ([Tumyto](https://tumyto.com/)).

### Inferences
- Make the Tiffin Pass hero’s first action check whether the office is in the service area; the fictional service only covers Andheri East and Powai, so availability is a more concrete first step than a generic “Get a pass.”
- Show one real-looking menu item with the cook, dish, price, and pickup/delivery timing near the CTA. Avoid using imagery or stats that look live unless they are real; otherwise label them as examples.
- Keep the three-view receipt interaction, but set “You” as the stable default and label concealed fields in visible text so the bars read as privacy masks, not loading placeholders.

### Gaps
- These references do not validate the proposed Tiffin Pass business rules or the current payment timeline. The page’s statements about escrow, charge timing, refunds, and kitchen payouts still need to agree with the actual service flow.

## Selection notes

### Takeaway
Use the references selectively. Local tiffin sites are closest to the product model; larger meal brands are useful for food photography, chef provenance, and the order journey, but their scale and positioning are different.

### Cited Findings
- Tumyto describes city-based local providers and location-dependent menus, while Chatori describes a marketplace serving both diners and home cooks ([Tumyto](https://tumyto.com/), [Chatori](https://chatori.app/)).
- CookUnity describes a chef-to-consumer prepared-meal service, while Thistle describes recurring prepared-meal deliveries and reusable delivery packaging ([CookUnity](https://www.cookunity.com/how-it-works), [Thistle](https://www.thistle.co/how/)).

### Inferences
- Recommended moodboard order: Maa Ka Khana for plan clarity; Chatori/Tumyto for local discovery and geography; CookUnity for food and kitchen storytelling; Thistle for the delivery/reuse sequence.
- Do not copy the sentimental “mother’s cooking” tropes or the aggressive promotional treatment visible on some commercial pages. Tiffin Pass’s more specific office-and-dabbawala story is its own differentiator.

### Gaps
- Awwwards-style visual portfolios and live browser screenshots were not part of this pass. Exact visual styling comparisons need a manual side-by-side review.
